# PART 0 — THE MISSION: WHY SERVYOU EXISTS AT A SCALE BIGGER THAN A MARKETPLACE

## The story we are actually telling

Servyou is not a website that sells things. Servyou is the infrastructure project for Tunisia's digital economy — and eventually North Africa's.

Here is the reality the foundation documents describe: Tunisia's digital economy exists, but it is homeless. Hundreds of thousands of freelancers, sellers, shop owners, and buyers transact every day — through Instagram DMs, Facebook Marketplace screenshots, WhatsApp voice notes, and paper notebooks. The economy is real. The infrastructure is not. Every existing option is either a foreign platform that doesn't understand dinars, governorates, COD, or Tunisian Arabic — or an improvised patchwork of social apps never designed for commerce. Jumia tried Tunisia and left. Amazon never came. The dinar's non-convertibility means most Tunisians literally cannot buy on international platforms even if they wanted to.

This is not a gap in the market. This is a gap in the **nation's economic infrastructure**. And the entity that fills it doesn't just win a market — it becomes the rails the digital economy runs on.

That is the scale of the story. Not "a Tunisian Etsy." Not "a local Fiverr." The story is: **Tunisia deserves a digital economy of its own, built on its own terms, in its own languages, with its own payment culture — and Servyou is building it.** First Tunisia, with density before breadth. Then the Maghreb, where Morocco, Algeria, and Libya share the same structural conditions: French-Arabic bilingualism, COD dominance, foreign-platform absence, young mobile-first populations underserved by global tech.

## The tone this mission demands

This mission changes the tone from "startup selling features" to **"national project inviting participation."** The voice doesn't beg for signups; it invites Tunisians to take part in building something that belongs to them. Every piece of copy, every section of the landing page, every social post carries this undertone: *this is ours, we're building it together, and it's about time.*

The mission is ambitious but the voice stays humble, per the locked vision in `product.md`: Servyou doesn't claim to replace everything; it earns its place as the most professional, modern, and secure platform in the market. Big ambition, calm delivery. The platform that leads a digital economy doesn't shout — it builds, shows, and invites.

## The three pillars under the mission (locked in product.md)

Everything Servyou says publicly maps to one of the three core values: **we protect our users** (safety beats convenience, safety beats profit), **we are honest and transparent, especially about money** (0% commission stated plainly, no hidden fees, no tricks), **we are genuinely Tunisian, not a copy of foreign platforms** (dinars, governorates, French + Arabic, COD as the norm not the workaround). These are not marketing slogans — they are decision rules, and the marketing's job is to make them visible.

# PART 1 — THE MIND OF THE TUNISIAN USER: THE PSYCHOLOGY THAT EVERYTHING IS BUILT ON

## The fear that runs the market

The 2024 Sfax University study quantified what every Tunisian seller already knows: **49.6% of Tunisian online shoppers fear their credit card information is at risk, and 88.9% fear receiving a faulty product.** Both are the same wound — the fear of being deceived by a stranger in a transaction where you must commit before you can verify. Add the documented spending reality (44.5% spend under 50 TND/month online) and the purchase drivers (special offers 37.2%, reduced prices 25.5%, free delivery 14.6%), and the picture is complete: the Tunisian buyer is price-conscious, fraud-cautious, quality-skeptical, and relationship-driven.

The Tunisian business culture research adds the second layer: **Tunisians prefer to do business with people they know and respect.** Trust precedes transaction. This is why WhatsApp vetting is in the MVP, why the founder note is on the landing page, why a phone number in the footer matters more than a contact form.

The implication is brutal and clarifying: **Servyou is not selling a marketplace. Servyou is selling trust.** The products and services are the inventory; trust is the actual offering. Every design, copy, and marketing decision gets tested against one question: *does this make a skeptical Tunisian feel safer, or not?*

## Cialdini's principles, mapped honestly to Servyou

The persuasion literature (Cialdini's seven principles — reciprocity, commitment, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity, unity) is the standard toolkit. The 2026 refinement of this literature matters more than the original list: **the principles work only when matched to the visitor's actual anxiety.** A page stacked with countdown timers and badges converts poorly if the visitor's real blocker is "is this for me?" Servyou's visitor anxieties are known (fraud, quality, legitimacy of the platform itself), so the mapping is precise:

**Unity is Servyou's strongest weapon.** Cialdini's seventh principle — shared identity — is the deepest lever available, and Servyou owns it structurally: *built in Tunisia, by a Tunisian, for Tunisians.* No foreign platform can copy this. Every "nous" in the copy, every governorate named, every dinar price shown activates unity. The mission narrative in Part 0 is unity at national scale.

**Liking through the founder.** A real founder with a real name and a real face, learning to code while building, telling the truth about what works and what's coming — this is the "liking" principle in its most honest form. The founder note section and About page are conversion infrastructure, not decoration.

**Social proof, honestly bootstrapped.** Day one, there are no five-figure user counts. The honest day-one proof: 24 governorates covered, 14 categories live, 0% commission, EU data hosting. Post-launch, the proof evolves: real testimonials with real names and cities ("Amira, Sfax"), aggregate completed-order counts from the admin statistics view (built for exactly this purpose per product.md). Never fake it — a fabricated testimonial discovered once destroys the entire trust offering.

**Authority through construction quality.** For a new platform, authority comes from *signals of construction*: the legal pages that read like a human wrote them, the accessibility statement, the honest cookie banner, the professional design system. Tunisians judge on appearance (documented in the business-culture research) — a world-class interface IS the authority signal.

**Reciprocity through genuine free value.** 0% commission at launch is structural reciprocity. The content strategy (Part 5) extends it: free educational content for sellers (how to photograph products, how to price, how to handle COD refusals) given before asking anything.

**Commitment through small steps.** Browse without an account (locked in the consumer journey) → first WhatsApp conversation → first request → first received order → upgrade to seller. Each small commitment makes the next natural. The landing page's job is only the first step.

**Scarcity, used sparingly and only when true.** Real stock counts ("Plus que 3 en stock") are fine. Manufactured urgency is banned by the voice rules — it reads as exactly the scam behavior Tunisians fear. The one legitimate scarcity play: founding-member framing during pre-launch ("Les 100 premières boutiques fondatrices").

## The three-second rule and what it means for everything

The conversion research is unambiguous: visitors decide whether to stay within roughly three seconds, and first impressions form in about fifty milliseconds. Color is processed before words, layout before sentences. This is why Part 2 (brand system) precedes Part 3 (landing page): the brand system IS the first three seconds. The headline gets read only by people the colors and layout already convinced not to leave.

# PART 2 — THE BRAND SYSTEM: IDENTITY, VOICE, MESSAGE, COLOR, TYPE

## The brand archetype (recommended for lock)

Servyou is **the young Tunisian builder** — the person who looked at the DM-and-screenshot economy and said "we deserve better, and I'm going to build it." Competent but not corporate. Warm but not naive. Local but operating at world standards. Honest but not preachy. This archetype is true (it is literally Moatez's story), which is why it works — archetypes fail when they're costumes.

Elevated by the Part 0 mission, the archetype scales: the young builder becomes the builder of national infrastructure. Same person, bigger canvas. The voice never changes; the stakes grow.

## The message hierarchy (recommended for lock)

**Primary positioning statement:** *"Une vraie maison pour l'économie tunisienne."* — The home metaphor, already proven in the hero. Emotional, memorable, scales with the mission (a home for the economy IS infrastructure language).

**Secondary line for short-form:** *"Vendez, achetez, travaillez — en Tunisien."* — Bios, footers, signup subtitles.

**The mission line (new, for the elevated narrative):** *"Nous construisons l'infrastructure de l'économie numérique tunisienne."* — For press, the About page, investor conversations, and moments when the full ambition needs to be stated plainly.

**Held in reserve:** *"Le marché tunisien qui fonctionne enfin."* — Deployed only when explicitly positioning against the failed alternatives (Jumia's exit, the DM economy). Confrontational; use strategically.

**The supporting message set** (deployed across landing page, FAQ, social, ads):
- Honest pricing: *"0% de commission au lancement. Pas de frais cachés. Ce que vous voyez est ce que vous payez."*
- Trust: *"Vos données restent en Europe, jamais revendues. Pas de pisteurs, pas de pièges."*
- Local: *"Construit en Tunisie, par un Tunisien, pour les Tunisiens."*
- Breadth: *"Boutiques et freelances. Produits et services. Tout en dinars."*
- Access: *"Paiement à la livraison. Disponible dans les 24 gouvernorats."*

## The five voice attributes (recommended for lock)

**Honest. Warm. Specific. Tunisien. Calm.** Detailed with sounds-like/sounds-wrong examples in `servyou-brand-foundation-research.md`. The operational summary: say what is true even when unflattering; talk like a knowledgeable friend, not a brochure; name concrete numbers and places, never vague claims; speak as a Tunisian platform, not a foreign one adapted; never shout, never manufacture urgency, never use superlatives without evidence. Banned: anglicisms where French words exist, exclamation-point marketing, blame-the-user error messages, promises the platform can't keep.

## Color (locked tokens, deployment strategy)

The two-blue system is locked: navy `#1E3A8A` (--brand-primary) and bright `#2563EB` (--brand-accent), with `#7DAEED` light accent, white and `#F8FAFC` surfaces, `#0F172A` / `#64748B` text. The research validates it: blue is the global trust color (healthcare sites using blue see 18% higher trust ratings; Stripe, PayPal, LinkedIn, Visa all live here), and the trust deficit in the Tunisian market makes blue the strategically correct family. Differentiation comes from the *duality* — navy carries weight and institutional seriousness, bright blue carries energy and action. One brand, two temperatures.

Deployment rhythm across the landing page (each section's background tells the emotional story before the words do): hero on soft blue-to-white gradient (Mediterranean, welcoming) → trust band on muted neutral with bright-blue numbers (factual confidence) → founder note on pure white (personal, premium) → problem section on light slate (acknowledging the pain) → benefits on white with per-card accents (energy) → journeys alternating white / soft blue (rhythm) → how-it-works on white with navy numbered circles (clarity) → FAQ on white (calm) → final CTA on deep navy with white text (conviction).

CTA color rule: bright blue `#2563EB` is the action color, used for primary CTAs and nowhere else in the same viewport, so it always wins the eye. Green `#10B981` stays reserved for success states in product UI only. No third marketing accent color — the two-blue identity is the differentiation.

## Typography (decision pending — the final recommendation)

The bilingual constraint rules everything: the French and Arabic scripts must share x-height, weight, and personality, or the brand breaks at every language switch. Two finalists survived the research, both free on Google Fonts, both loadable via `next/font/google`:

**Tajawal** (Boutros Fonts; Arabic + Latin designed simultaneously, 7 weights) — warm, modern, geometric-friendly. The marketplace-warmth choice.

**IBM Plex Sans + IBM Plex Sans Arabic** (designed as one family by IBM/Bold Monday) — institutional, premium, serious. The infrastructure-authority choice.

**Final recommendation: Tajawal for the marketing and consumer surfaces, with the option to revisit as the platform matures.** The reasoning: the brand voice is warm-first ("a knowledgeable friend"), the audience is consumers and small sellers (not enterprises), and the Inter-monoculture research (Inter appears on 182 of 500+ surveyed SaaS sites — using it means invisibility) argues for a font with regional character. Tajawal's Arabic is widely regarded as among the most beautiful open-source Arabic faces, which matters enormously for the RTL experience that half the audience will live in. The mission's "infrastructure authority" is carried by the navy color, the construction quality, and the copy — the type can afford to be warm. **The pick is Moatez's call;** if the brand should feel more like a bank than a market, IBM Plex wins instead. (The currently-shipped Inter is a fine placeholder; the swap is a one-line change in the root layout when the decision locks.)

Type ramp principle from the research: scale dramatically. Hero H1 at 56-72px desktop / 32-40px mobile, weight 700-800 (one step heavier than the rest of the page — the hero must win the eye). Body at 16-18px, line-height 1.6. The 4-5x scale between H1 and body is what reads as confidence; timid 2x scales read as amateur. Arabic gets the +20% horizontal expansion buffer in every headline container.

# PART 3 — THE LANDING PAGE MASTER BLUEPRINT

The landing page is the highest-leverage marketing surface: a page converting 30% of arrivals is worth six times a page converting 5%, at identical build cost. All traffic — social, organic, word-of-mouth, future paid — converges here. The architecture below is locked at nine sections; the visual treatment iterates, the architecture doesn't. Every section lists its psychological job, its content, and copy direction. Build order: top to bottom, one section per commit on `design/landing-page-hero`, no merge until the whole page (plus mobile pass, plus RTL pass) is complete — per the locked merge rule.

## Section 1 — Hero *(built; iterate to final)*

**Psychological job:** survive the three-second test and make the visitor feel, before reading anything: *this is serious, this is modern, and this is ours.* Unity + authority in fifty milliseconds.

**Structure (locked):** eyebrow label "PLATEFORME TUNISIENNE" in brand-accent → H1 *"Une vraie maison pour l'économie tunisienne"* → subheadline naming dinars, COD, bilingual, EU data → primary CTA *"Créer un compte gratuit"* (bright blue, the only bright-blue element in the viewport) + ghost secondary *"Découvrir la plateforme"* → right column: phone mockup with three real-feeling order cards (sac en cuir artisanal 45 TND · Tunis, design de logo 120 TND · Sami K. Sfax, développement site web 1500 TND · DevTunisia) with three floating badges (0% commission, 24 gouvernorats, Tunisien à 100%).

**Why the mockup matters:** the 2026 hero standard is story-driven product demonstration, not stock photos or abstract art. The order cards ARE the story — real Tunisian products, real dinar prices, real cities, real lifecycle statuses (Demande envoyée, Acceptée, En cours). The visitor sees the platform working before reading a word. Post-launch upgrade path: replace the static mockup with a subtle animation showing the scattered-DMs-to-organized-dashboard transformation (the strategic reference's ranked concept #1).

**Mobile rule:** single column, headline + CTA within the first screen height, mockup below. Mobile is the primary experience (70%+ of Tunisian traffic), not the shrunk version.

## Section 2 — Trust band *(next to build)*

**Psychological job:** answer "is this real?" with verifiable numbers within the first scroll. Honest day-one social proof.

**Content:** one full-width band, four hard claims: **24 gouvernorats couverts · 14 catégories actives · 0% de commission · Données hébergées en Europe.** Numbers in bright blue at heavy weight, labels in muted text. No logos (there are none yet — never fake them), no vague claims. Post-launch, this band evolves: add real aggregate counts from the admin statistics view ("1 247 commandes complétées") the moment they're respectable.

## Section 3 — Founder note *(built; iterate to final)*

**Psychological job:** liking + accountability. In a relationship-first business culture, an identifiable human behind the platform is worth more than any badge. This is the anti-Jumia signal: not an anonymous foreign entity, a Tunisian you could message.

**Content:** centered, white background, avatar (MZ gradient placeholder until a real photo is ready — the real photo is worth a measurable trust lift, founder's call), label FONDATEUR, name, and an italic quote in the founder's actual voice. Copy direction: *"J'ai construit Servyou parce que notre économie mérite mieux que des captures d'écran et des DM perdus. Une plateforme tunisienne, sérieuse, honnête — c'est possible. La voici."* Short, first-person, no corporate polish.

## Section 4 — Problem agitation

**Psychological job:** empathy through recognition. The visitor must think *"they understand exactly my life"* before any benefit will land. PAS framework, agitation kept warm, never lecturing.

**Content:** a short headline naming the current reality — *"Vendre en Tunisie aujourd'hui, c'est ça :"* — followed by three tight recognition vignettes, one per audience: the shop owner with orders scattered across Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp and TikTok and a paper notebook that connects to nothing; the freelancer delivering work and chasing payment from clients who vanish; the buyer staring at a product photo wondering if the seller is real and the product matches. Close with the turn: *"Ce n'est pas normal. Et ce n'est plus obligatoire."* Light slate background — the one visually somber section, by design.

## Section 5 — Three benefits

**Psychological job:** translate the three core values into visitor outcomes. Values → benefits, claim → proof.

**Content:** three cards, each with icon, short headline, one-line claim, one concrete proof. **Tunisien d'abord** — *"Construit en Tunisie, par un Tunisien, pour les Tunisiens. Dinars, gouvernorats, paiement à la livraison — pas une adaptation, une origine."* **Honnête sur les prix** — *"0% de commission au lancement. Pas de frais cachés, pas de surprises. Tout changement sera annoncé clairement, jamais imposé."* **Vos données, votre maison** — *"Hébergées en Europe, jamais revendues. Pas de pisteurs tiers, pas de publicité ciblée. Votre vie privée n'est pas notre produit."*

## Section 6 — Three journeys

**Psychological job:** the Stripe multi-audience pattern — speak to each side directly instead of averaging them into mush. Each audience must find *their* section and see *their* first week on the platform. This is also where cross-side network effects get planted: buyers see there are sellers; sellers see there are buyers.

**Content:** three alternating image-left/right rows, each with label, headline, two-sentence journey, mini-mockup of the actual product surface, and an audience-framed CTA. **Pour les boutiques** (supply side first): list products → share the shop link in social bios → COD requests arrive organized in one dashboard → manage the 8-stage lifecycle instead of the notebook. CTA: *"Créer ma boutique."* **Pour les freelances:** build a profile → list services → browse the job board → vet clients on WhatsApp before committing. CTA: *"Créer mon profil freelance."* **Pour les acheteurs:** browse without an account → check full seller profiles → request via WhatsApp → pay cash at delivery, confirm receipt in the app. CTA: *"Explorer les boutiques."* The mini-mockups are the "show don't tell" half — real product surfaces, not illustrations.

## Section 7 — How it works

**Psychological job:** reduce the unfamiliarity cost. The commitment principle in sequence — four small, imaginable steps.

**Content:** four numbered steps with navy circles, action verbs, present tense: **1. Créez votre compte gratuit** (30 secondes, sans carte bancaire) → **2. Parcourez ou publiez** (boutiques, services, missions) → **3. Échangez et concluez** (WhatsApp pour vérifier, paiement à la livraison) → **4. Confirmez et construisez votre réputation** (chaque commande complétée renforce votre profil). The "sans carte bancaire" detail is load-bearing — it directly neutralizes the 49.6% credit-card fear at the exact moment the visitor considers acting.

## Section 8 — FAQ

**Psychological job:** handle the objections of the careful visitor — the one who scrolled this far IS the high-intent visitor. Honest answers to the actual fears, in the user's phrasing, accordion pattern.

**The seven questions (from Pillar 4 user research + the strategic reference):** *Comment Servyou empêche-t-il les arnaques ?* (profiles, moderation, reports, dispute resolution with lifecycle evidence — and honest acknowledgment that WhatsApp vetting + COD means you never pay before seeing). *Et si le produit livré n'est pas ce que j'ai commandé ?* (refuse delivery, pay nothing, report — the COD advantage stated plainly). *Servyou prend-il une commission ?* (0% at launch; any future change announced clearly in advance). *Mes données sont-elles vendues ?* (never; EU-hosted; no third-party trackers). *Pourquoi français et arabe, pas anglais ?* (built for how Tunisians actually live; English later if needed). *Qui peut vendre ?* (18+, shops or freelancers, real signup information required). *Comment Servyou gagne de l'argent alors ?* — answer this honestly (free at launch to build the economy first; sustainable revenue comes later and will be announced transparently). The platform brave enough to answer the money question wins the careful visitor permanently.

## Section 9 — Final CTA + Footer

**Psychological job:** convert the convinced. One emotional anchor, one action, zero distraction.

**Content:** deep navy section, white text. The remembered line: *"La Tunisie mérite une vraie place de marché. Nous la construisons. Rejoignez-nous."* Primary CTA *"Créer un compte gratuit — en 30 secondes"* with the trust micro-band repeated underneath (Paiement à la livraison · 0% commission · Données en Europe). Footer per the strategic reference: logo + tagline, secondary nav (À propos, Contact, FAQ), legal links (CGU, Confidentialité, Cookies, Accessibilité), language toggle, social links (Instagram + Facebook minimum), copyright + legal entity, and — the Tunisia-specific credibility detail — a WhatsApp contact link, because Tunisians trust platforms where a real human is reachable.

**Conversion note for later:** when paid acquisition starts, paid-traffic landing variants strip the top navigation entirely (logo only) — removing nav on paid landers measurably lifts conversion. The organic homepage keeps its nav.

# PART 4 — THE MARKETING STRATEGY: FROM ZERO TO THE FLYWHEEL

## The strategic spine: supply first, density first, trust always

The cold-start literature (Andrew Chen et al.) and every surviving marketplace converge on the same sequence, and Servyou's foundation docs already encode it. The plan below organizes it into phases.

**Supply first.** Demand won't arrive without supply worth browsing. Servyou's structural advantage: supply acquisition is nearly free, because shop owners and freelancers bring their existing social audiences with them (the social-bio link strategy locked in product.md). The platform doesn't have to buy demand — every seller who joins imports their own.

**Density first.** Launch Tunis–Ariana–Ben Arous, not 24 governorates at once: 35% of the population, highest internet penetration, highest concentration of active social-commerce sellers. The landing page says "24 gouvernorats" (the platform covers them); the marketing *spend and effort* concentrates on one cluster until liquidity is real. Density activates network effects: buyers find sellers nearby, delivery is cheap, word of mouth travels through real social graphs.

**The metric is liquidity, not signups.** Track activated buyers (≥1 completed COD request) and active sellers (≥1 request received in trailing 14 days). A hundred users with twenty weekly transactions beats a thousand users with ten. Vanity volume is the trap.

## Phase 1 — Pre-launch (now → launch): build the audience before the doors open

The launch playbook research is unanimous: launch is a 90-day campaign, not a day, and pre-launch determines launch success. You cannot manufacture momentum from zero on launch day.

**The founding-hundred program.** Hand-recruit the first 100 sellers (shops + freelancers) one conversation at a time — DMs to active Tunisian social-commerce sellers, personal briefings, direct WhatsApp access to Moatez for their first weeks. Frame it as status, honestly: *"Boutique fondatrice de Servyou"* / *"Freelance fondateur"* — founding-member badges that persist on their profiles forever. This is Superhuman's scarcity-as-positioning, except the scarcity is real (there can only ever be 100 founders). These hundred conversations are also the user-research substrate the platform's evolution feeds on. Resist automating this.

**The public build narrative.** Moatez builds in public on Instagram, TikTok, and Facebook: the accounting-background founder learning to code, building national infrastructure, showing real screens as they come alive. Build-in-public content is the highest-leverage pre-launch content for a solo founder because it compounds liking + authority + unity simultaneously, costs nothing but consistency, and creates the launch-day audience. (This sequencing aligns with the locked launch order in the roadmap: design system → brandbook → visual identity → social accounts → marketing strategy → posts + videos → ads → launch.)

**The waitlist with a reason.** A simple pre-launch capture on the landing page: *"Soyez parmi les premiers — accès prioritaire au lancement."* The waitlist research shows the nurture content matters more than the capture: weekly value (seller tips, build updates, founding-member spotlights), not promotional fatigue.

## Phase 2 — Launch window (the first 30 days): the concierge launch

Open to the founding hundred plus waitlist, concentrated in the Tunis cluster. Moatez personally responds to every email and WhatsApp within hours — the behavior-signal research is stark (60% of consumers expect responses within 10 minutes; the user who gets a real founder reply at 8am tells their friends, and early word-of-mouth outperforms any paid spend). Every founding seller gets help with their first listing. Every early dispute gets resolved fast and visibly fairly. The first 90 days of platform behavior ARE the brand; no campaign can overwrite them.

Launch-week content: the founder's launch video (the mission, the story, the invitation), founding-seller spotlights (their shops, their products, their links — making them the heroes makes them amplifiers), and the press note to Tunisian tech media framed on the Part 0 narrative — *Tunisian founder builds the infrastructure for Tunisia's digital economy* — which is a story journalists can run, where "new marketplace launches" is not.

## Phase 3 — Post-launch (months 2-6): the flywheel

The growth engine is already designed into the product: **sellers share their Servyou links in social bios and content → their followers click through and become Servyou users → those buyers discover other sellers on the platform → cross-side network effects compound.** Marketing's job is to fuel this loop, not replace it: make sellers successful (education content, featured spotlights), make their links attractive (beautiful public shop/profile pages — which is exactly what the design phase is building), and surface aggregate proof as it accumulates (completed-order milestones from the admin stats view: "1 000 commandes complétées" is a campaign in itself).

Paid acquisition waits until the loop demonstrably works (per the launch-strategy research: paid scales proven channels, it doesn't create product-market fit). When it starts: Facebook + Instagram ads concentrated on the Tunis cluster, COD-fear-neutralizing creative ("payez seulement quand vous voyez le produit"), pointed at stripped-nav landing variants.

**The expansion sequence (the mission arc):** Tunis cluster liquidity → Sfax and Sousse (the next two cities in the 98%-of-transactions data) → national density → and only then the Maghreb conversation. Each expansion repeats the density playbook. The mission is the destination; density discipline is the route.

# PART 5 — THE CONTENT STRATEGY: WHAT SERVYOU SAYS EVERY WEEK

## The platform reality (2025-2026 data)

Over 70% of new African e-commerce users arrive via social platforms, led by TikTok and Instagram; Facebook remains dominant for older demographics and local commerce communities in Tunisia; TikTok Shop's trajectory (≈20% of US social commerce, doubling yearly) shows where social commerce is heading globally. For Servyou: **Instagram is the brand home, TikTok is the growth engine, Facebook is the community and trust layer.** Same mission, platform-native execution — never the same video reposted thrice.

## The four content pillars

**Pillar 1 — La Mission (≈20%).** The national-infrastructure narrative: build-in-public updates, founder story chapters, "why Tunisia deserves its own platform" essays-as-carousels, milestone announcements. This pillar carries the Part 0 tone and differentiates Servyou from every dropshipping page in the feed.

**Pillar 2 — Les Vendeurs (≈30%).** Seller education and seller heroes: how to photograph products with a phone, how to price for COD, how to handle refusals, how to write a service offer that wins missions — plus founding-seller spotlights that make real Tunisians the protagonists. This pillar is reciprocity at scale and recruits supply continuously.

**Pillar 3 — La Confiance (≈25%).** Trust mechanics made visible: how the 8-stage order lifecycle protects both sides, what "your data stays in Europe" actually means, how disputes get resolved, why COD-plus-confirmation beats pay-before-you-see. Each piece neutralizes one documented fear. This is the pillar competitors can't copy because their mechanics don't support it.

**Pillar 4 — Le Marché (≈25%).** The living economy: products and services on the platform, category highlights, city spotlights ("les boutiques de Sfax"), trending items, freelancer skills in demand. Post-launch this becomes increasingly user-generated — sellers' own content, re-shared with credit, which is both free inventory and the strongest social proof.

## Platform playbooks

**Instagram:** the polished home. Carousels for education (Pillar 2-3), Reels for mission and spotlight stories, Stories for daily build-in-public, bio link to the landing page. Brand visual system applied strictly — the feed is the brandbook in public.

**TikTok:** raw and native. Hook in the first 3 seconds (the research's "hook economy"), founder-to-camera mission clips, before/after seller stories (notebook → dashboard), Tunisian Arabic (Darija) welcome here for warmth — this is where the brand speaks most casually. Consistency requirement: 3-5 posts weekly minimum; TikTok rewards rhythm over polish.

**Facebook:** community and reach into the demographic that does its commerce in Facebook groups. Page mirrors Instagram's key content; the real play is group participation — Tunisian buy/sell groups are exactly where the scattered economy lives today, and helpful (never spammy) presence there reaches users at the moment of their pain.

**Language strategy:** French-first for educational and platform content (commerce convention), Darija for warmth moments and TikTok, MSA Arabic for the formal Arabic surface. Every campaign asset exists in French + Arabic; the 20% expansion rule applies to every text overlay.

## The weekly rhythm (sustainable for a solo founder)

Three to five posts weekly across platforms, built from one weekly content batch: one pillar-1 or pillar-2 anchor piece (carousel or video, repurposed natively per platform), two lighter pieces (story-style updates, spotlights, market highlights), plus daily Stories during active build periods. The repurposing discipline from the research: one anchor idea → IG carousel + TikTok talking-head + FB post. Consistency beats volume; the brand that posts three good pieces every week for a year beats the brand that posts daily for six weeks and vanishes.

# PART 6 — MEASUREMENT: WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE

**Landing page:** signup conversion rate (target band from research: a good marketplace landing page converts 10-30% of intent-driven traffic; measure, iterate sections against it), scroll depth past the trust band, CTA click-through by section. One change at a time, measured.

**Marketplace (the six-month definition locked in product.md):** supply is real (active shops and freelancers with real listings), people find what they need (steady growth in requests created), deals actually happen (growing `received` confirmations — real money, real problems solved), people come back (second and third visits, referral arrivals). Liquidity over vanity, always: activated buyers and 14-day-active sellers are the two numbers on the wall.

**Content:** follower quality over quantity (Tunisian audience share, seller-audience share), link-through to the landing page, founding-program applications. The content's job is to feed the funnel, and the funnel's job is liquidity.
